1. What is an ETF?

Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) are baskets of stocks that track an index (e.g., Nifty 50, Nifty Auto). They trade like stocks but give mutual-fund style diversification.

Low cost

Expense ratios typically 0.05% – 0.3%.

Diversified

Own 50+ stocks in one click.

Liquid

Buy/sell anytime during market hours.

2. ETFs vs picking individual stocks

Unless you have hours daily to research, concentrated stock bets can ruin compounding. ETFs smooth out the ride.

Individual Stocks ETFs
Time required High – tracking quarterly results Low – index rebalances automatically
Risk of blowups High – 1 bad stock hurts portfolio Low – diversified basket
Costs Brokerage + slippage per stock Single transaction, very low fee
3. ETFs vs mutual funds (2026)

Mutual funds are still great, but ETFs win on cost and transparency.

  • Fees: Active equity mutual funds average 1.5% expense ratio; ETFs ~0.15%.
  • Transparency: ETFs publish holdings daily, mutual funds monthly.
  • Taxation: Both share same capital gains rules, but ETFs avoid exit loads.
  • Execution: Mutual funds execute end-of-day; ETFs trade instantly, letting SmartETF catch dips.
